Understanding your patterns

Exploratory analysis

How CARESYNQ explores patterns

Our prototype explores statistical patterns in submitted questionnaire responses and activity records. A pattern is not a diagnosis or a clinical risk classification.

01 · Collection

Voluntary activity and questionnaire information is recorded.

02 · Preparation

Data is checked, cleaned and prepared for analysis.

03 · Pattern discovery

Statistical methods explore similarities and differences in responses.

04 · Human support

Any concern should be discussed with an appropriate healthcare professional.

The current questionnaire clustering model is exploratory. It does not predict postpartum depression and is not clinically validated.

Understanding your data

Activity information

Activity entries can include sessions, minutes spent, feeding-log counts and sleep-log counts.

Wellbeing information

Your selected mood, optional note and contact request are recorded when you submit a check-in.

Pattern information

Statistical patterns are exploratory and must not be interpreted as medical conclusions.

Privacy and responsible use

Your information deserves protection.

CARESYNQ is a software prototype. Only submit information you are comfortable sharing. The prototype should use appropriate access controls, secure storage and consent procedures before any real-world use.

  • Do not enter passwords or highly sensitive personal information in notes.
  • Activity tracking is optional.
  • Do not treat missing entries as evidence of a health condition.
  • Clinical use requires appropriate consent, governance and validation.

Need someone to talk to? ♡

If you feel persistently low, anxious, overwhelmed or concerned about your wellbeing, consider speaking with a qualified healthcare professional.

If you feel unsafe or are in immediate danger, seek urgent local emergency assistance or go to the nearest emergency department.

Important: CARESYNQ is not a medical service.

This website is an exploratory software prototype. It does not diagnose postpartum depression, calculate a clinically validated depression probability, or replace professional assessment. Statistical patterns are not clinical risk groups. All care decisions must remain with the individual and qualified professionals.
